# :fl-deadlock: Conflicts and deadlock

!!! abstract "The question"

    What exactly does flatland forbid when two trains want the same track, and why is a deadlock
    forever?

## One cell, one train: MotionCheck

In every step each train asks for one cell: the one it is in (to stay) or the next one (to move).
Flatland's `MotionCheck` then decides who actually moves
([`agent_chains.py`](https://github.com/flatland-association/flatland-rl/blob/v4.3.0/flatland/envs/agent_chains.py),
after Bochatay 2024):

- **No two trains in one cell.** If several trains want the same cell, the one with the lowest
  index wins and the others are stopped, and so is every train queued behind a stopped one.
- **No swaps.** Two neighbouring trains that want each other's cells are both stopped: trains
  cannot pass through each other.
- **Following is fine.** A train may enter a cell in the same step its occupant leaves it, so a
  queue of trains at the same speed moves as a block.
- **Rings rotate.** Three or more trains that each want the next one's cell, in a closed ring,
  all move together.

The Scheduling Lab's JavaScript port reproduces these rules, including the order in which Python
iterates its set of stopped trains, which decides the outcome when several are stopped at once.

## Deadlock

Put two trains nose to nose on single track. Each wants the other's cell, which would be a swap,
so neither moves. Neither can reverse. Nothing will ever change: a **deadlock** is absorbing. Every
train that later needs either cell, or queues behind the pair, is stuck too, so deadlocks spread.

<div class="fl-widget" data-widget="deadlock" data-title="Interactive: two trains, one passing loop"></div>

The decision that prevents a deadlock is not taken where the trains meet. It is taken at the switch
before the single-track section, or at the station, when one train must accept to wait for the
other. In the widget, one train taking the loop is enough because the timing happens to work, but
both trains taking it meet head-on inside the loop. Whether a train must also wait there depends
on where the other train will be several cells later.

## Counting deadlocked trains

`rl_flatland.deadlock.find_deadlocked` reports the trains that will never move: start from every
train whose every possible next cell is occupied, keep only those blocked by trains in the same set
(a greatest fixed point), find the head-on pairs among them, and add every train queued behind a
pair. A train waiting behind a *broken* train is not deadlocked: the broken one will move on.

One case it gets wrong, found while building the dispatcher on page 8: four trains in a 2×2 block of
switches can sit in each other's successor cells while their plan rotates them one cell each in a
single step, which flatland allows. The detector flags two of them as a head-on pair. In this
repository's runs that happened on 12 of the 40 held-out large and xlarge episodes
([TADA on rails, what did not work](../08-tada-dispatcher.md#what-did-not-work)); the dispatcher
therefore only terminates on a flagged set whose planned moves really are a swap.

!!! tip "What it means for the agent"

    - **An absorbing failure state, decided early.** Entering a single-track segment is safe or
      fatal depending on where other trains will be ten to fifty steps later. Credit for the
      mistake arrives long after the action, and the episode keeps running with the damage.
    - **A coordination game at every segment.** Two trains at opposite ends of a segment both do
      well by entering if the other waits, and both lose everything if neither waits. Independent
      learners tend to learn to go.
    - **Index order is a hidden priority.** When two trains want the same free cell, the lower
      index wins. A policy that ignores this can be surprised; a planner uses it deliberately.

??? question "Check yourself"

    1. Two trains on adjacent cells face each other on single track. Can `STOP_MOVING` by one of
       them resolve it? *No. Stopping keeps it in its cell; the other still cannot enter. Only a
       train that never entered the segment could have helped.*
    2. Three trains form a ring and each wants the next one's cell. Do they move? *Yes, flatland
       rotates a ring of three or more in one step; only a ring of two, a swap, is forbidden.*
    3. Why is a train behind a broken-down train not counted as deadlocked? *The broken train
       resumes when its counter reaches zero, so the queue behind it will move again.*
